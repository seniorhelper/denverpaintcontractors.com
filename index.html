Protect yourself first

Seven questions to ask any Denver painter before you sign

Colorado doesn't issue a statewide painting license, so the checking is up to you. Ask every painter these, including Bighorn.

  1. Can I see your certificate of insurance?Liability coverage protects you if someone gets hurt or something gets damaged on your property.
  2. Is prep written into the estimate?Washing, scraping, sanding, caulking and priming should be spelled out, not implied.
  3. Which paint line and sheen, on which surfaces?"Premium paint" means nothing. A product name on paper means something.
  4. How many coats?Coverage and film build drive how long the job lasts at altitude.
  5. Who is on my job every day?You should know who runs the crew and who to call.
  6. My home is pre-1978. How do you handle lead paint?Federal EPA rules require certified firms and lead-safe practices when older painted surfaces are disturbed.
  7. What happens if something fails after you leave?Get the guarantee in writing and ask how a callback actually works.

Pricing, plainly

What house painting costs in Denver, and why quotes vary so much

Two houses with the same square footage can land thousands of dollars apart. These are the things that actually move a Denver painting quote.

  • Height and storiesTwo-story walls, dormers and steep lots need more ladder and staging time.
  • Surface conditionPeeling, chalking, failed caulk and bare wood add scraping, sanding and priming.
  • Trim and detailOlder Denver bungalows and Victorians carry far more trim per wall.
  • Paint lineA top-tier coating costs more per gallon and usually earns it back in longevity.
  • Color changeGoing dark to light, or light to deep, can add a coat.
  • Home agePre-1978 homes may need lead-safe work practices.

Ballpark numbers

Angi's Denver cost data puts exterior house painting at roughly $1.55 to $4.10 per square foot. Interior projects are commonly quoted between $1.50 and $3.50 per square foot.

Those are market ranges, not Bighorn's price list. The only number that means anything is one based on your walls, which is why Bighorn estimates in person, in writing, for free.

Straight answers

Questions Denver homeowners ask before hiring a painter

How much does it cost to paint a house in Denver?

Angi's Denver cost data puts exterior house painting at roughly $1.55 to $4.10 per square foot, and interior projects are commonly quoted between $1.50 and $3.50 per square foot. Where your home lands depends on stories and ladder height, how much scraping, caulking and priming the surface needs, trim detail, and the paint line. Bighorn Painting gives free written estimates in person so the number reflects your actual house. Call 720-912-4676 to schedule one.

Is Bighorn Painting licensed and insured?

Bighorn Painting is insured and brings proof of coverage to every estimate. Colorado does not issue a statewide painting license, and local contractor licensing rules vary by city, so ask any painter you interview, including us, for a certificate of insurance and any license the city you live in requires before work starts.

What paint brands does Bighorn Painting use?

Bighorn primarily uses Sherwin-Williams and PPG, and matches the product line to the surface and its sun exposure. The product and sheen are written on the estimate, and a cheaper can is never swapped in after you sign. If you already have a brand or color in mind, bring it up at the estimate.

What is your satisfaction guarantee?

A job is not finished until you walk it with the crew and say it is right. If something needs correcting after that, Bighorn comes back and makes it right. No honest painter can promise a flawless first pass, but Bighorn promises to be there to fix it. The coatings also carry their own manufacturer warranties on the paint itself.

How long does an exterior paint job take in Denver?

Most residential exteriors take 3 to 5 working days, depending on the size of the home, number of stories, how much prep the surface needs, and the weather. Colorado forecasts change fast, so the crew schedules around proper drying conditions between coats instead of racing a storm.

When is the best time of year to paint a house exterior in Denver?

Late spring through early fall gives the most reliable exterior painting days along the Front Range. Every paint label lists a minimum application temperature, and the overnight low matters as much as the afternoon high, so the crew watches both. Popular summer weeks book up, which is why homeowners who call in late winter or early spring usually get the dates they want.

Can you paint interiors during the winter?

Yes. Interior painting runs year-round because the work happens inside a heated home. Winter is a good time to schedule bedrooms, living areas, ceilings, trim and cabinets, and it frees up the warm months for exterior work.

Do you paint kitchen cabinets in Denver?

Yes. Cabinets are degreased, scuff-sanded, primed with a bonding primer, and sprayed with a cabinet-grade finish so the result is smooth with no brush marks. Doors and drawer fronts are typically finished in a controlled area while the boxes are finished in place with full containment.

Do you offer deck and fence staining?

Yes. Decks and fences are cleaned, prepped, and stained with products chosen to stand up to Colorado sun and freeze-thaw cycles. Bighorn works on cedar, pine and pressure-treated wood, and stains railings, pergolas and gates to match.

My Denver home was built before 1978. Does that change the job?

It can. Homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint, and the federal EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule requires certified firms and lead-safe work practices when painted surfaces in those homes are disturbed. Mention the year your home was built when you request a quote so the estimate accounts for it.
