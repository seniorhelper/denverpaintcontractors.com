Brick and masonry

Should You Paint a Brick House in Denver?

About half of Denver's homes are brick, thanks to a fire in 1863. Most of that brick should never see a paint roller. Some of it should. Here's how to tell which kind of wall you have.

By Denver Paint ContractorsPublished October 25, 202610 minute read

Short answer

If your brick has never been painted and it's sound, usually don't paint it: clean it, repoint it and paint the trim. If it's already painted, keep it painted with a breathable masonry coating. Fix any moisture first. In a Denver landmark or historic district, check with Landmark Preservation before painting unpainted brick. Angi puts brick painting in Denver at $1.55 to $4.60 per sq ft.

Why are so many Denver houses brick?

Because of a fire. Early on April 19, 1863, a fire started in downtown Denver and burned through the mostly wooden business district. The Denver Public Library's history of the Great Fire describes a town that rebuilt in brick. The city passed an ordinance requiring new buildings to be brick or stone. The area had plenty of clay, so brick became the default. Colorado Public Radio, quoting a library archivist, reported the rule stayed on the books until around 1960.

The result is still on every block. Data from Denver's Department of Finance, reported in 2025, shows that almost 53% of the city's residential properties are brick or clad in brick. Bungalows in Washington Park, Tudors in Park Hill, foursquares in Capitol Hill, post-war ranches in Harvey Park: much of Denver's housing stock is masonry. So the question we hear most from brick-home owners isn't what color to choose. It's whether to paint at all.

Should you paint a brick house in Denver?

Usually not, if it has never been painted. Usually yes, if it already is. That's the honest short version, and here's why.

Unpainted brick takes almost no upkeep. A fired clay brick has a hard outer face that sheds water. Some moisture always gets into a masonry wall, and bare brick lets it dry back out through the face and the mortar joints. Put the wrong coating over it and that drying slows down. Water that can't get out freezes in the brick. Denver goes through freeze-thaw cycles all winter (our Denver painting data page has the NOAA freeze counts), and every cycle pushes on the trapped water until the face of the brick pops off. Masons call that spalling, and you can't paint it away.

Once brick is painted, though, getting back to bare brick is expensive and hard on the masonry. The Brick Industry Association's older guidance on painting brick warned that painted exterior masonry needs repainting every three to five years. Modern masonry coatings do better than that, but painted brick never goes back to zero maintenance. That's the real trade: color now, in exchange for a coating you'll maintain for as long as you own the house.

Can you paint brick in a Denver historic district?

Check before you buy a gallon. Denver's design guidelines for landmark structures and historic districts cover individually designated landmarks and every historic district except Lower Downtown, which has its own guidelines. The updated guidelines, adopted by the Landmark Preservation Commission in February 2026, tell owners to keep masonry's original protective surface and not to paint masonry that was never painted. If your house is a designated landmark or sits in one of Denver's historic districts, talk to Denver Landmark Preservation before you change unpainted brick. Repainting brick that's already painted is a different conversation from painting it for the first time, and staff can tell you which applies to you.

HOAs are the other gatekeeper. Many newer communities treat brick wainscots and accents as part of the approved exterior and won't let you paint them. Our HOA paint approval guide has a request-letter tool if you need to ask.

Paint, limewash or stain: what are the options for brick?

There are four ways to change how a brick house looks, and they behave very differently over time.

  • Masonry paint (breathable acrylic). A full, opaque color change. Use a coating made for masonry and rated vapor-permeable, over a masonry primer where the label calls for one. Don't use ordinary house paint, and never use a non-breathable elastomeric on brick that gets wet from behind. It's the most dramatic change and the most maintenance.
  • Mineral (silicate) paint. It soaks into the masonry instead of sitting on top, so it stays very breathable and holds its color well in strong sun. It only bonds to bare or mineral surfaces, so it's for unpainted brick or brick stripped back to clean masonry.
  • Limewash. Lime and pigment brushed on, often washed back for an aged look. Very breathable, and it fades and wears unevenly over the years, which is the point. Expect to refresh it rather than repaint it.
  • Masonry stain. Penetrating and translucent to semi-solid. It shifts the color of red or orange brick toward brown, gray or charcoal and keeps the texture and the mortar lines. It's the most reversible-looking choice and the lowest maintenance of the four.

Whichever you choose, the prep is the same. Wash with a masonry-safe cleaner, not an acid wash (brick makers warn that acidic cleaners cause paint failures). Let the wall dry fully. Repoint open mortar joints. Fix the water sources first: gutters, downspouts, sprinklers aimed at the wall, missing caps on top of walls and chimneys. New masonry, such as a fresh repair or an addition, needs to cure first. One brick maker's painting guide recommends at least 30 days, then a check for moisture or efflorescence.

What does it cost to paint a brick house in Denver?

Angi's Denver exterior painting data puts brick at $1.55 to $4.60 per square foot, the widest range of any siding it lists. That's because brick drinks paint and takes longer to cut in around mortar joints. Stripping old paint, where it's needed, runs $0.50 to $2.05 per square foot on top. On a typical one-story Denver brick ranch with about 1,300 sq ft of masonry wall, that's roughly $2,000 to $6,000 to paint before any repointing, and far more if the old paint has to come off. A masonry stain or limewash on bare brick usually lands lower than full paint, because there's no primer and a single application often does it.

The mistakes we fix most on painted brick

  1. Painting over efflorescence. The salts keep coming and push the paint off from behind within a season or two.
  2. Non-breathable paint or elastomeric on a wet wall. It blisters, then peels in sheets, and sometimes takes the face of the brick with it.
  3. Skipping the repointing. Paint doesn't fix open joints. Water gets in behind the paint, and the paint fails along every joint line.
  4. Pressure washing at close range. It cuts into soft mortar and old brick faces and drives water deep into the wall right before painting.
  5. Painting too late in the year. Masonry stays cold and holds moisture. Brick should be dry and above the coating's label minimum temperature, and it should stay that way while the coating cures.

The bottom line

Denver is a brick city because of an 1863 fire, and most of that brick is happiest left alone. If yours is bare and sound, clean it, repoint it and paint the trim. If it's already painted, keep it painted with a breathable masonry coating, and fix the water before you recoat. And if you're in a historic district, call Landmark Preservation first. Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Is it a bad idea to paint brick?

On sound, unpainted brick it usually is. It adds a maintenance cycle and can trap moisture that freeze-thaw turns into spalling. Painting makes sense when the brick is already painted, badly mismatched from old repairs, or when you want a look that stain or limewash can't give. Then use a breathable masonry coating and fix any water problems first.

How long does paint last on brick in Colorado?

It depends on the coating and on moisture more than on the paint brand. The Brick Industry Association's older guidance said painted exterior masonry needs repainting every three to five years. Good modern masonry coatings on a dry, well-prepped wall last longer, but south- and west-facing walls in Denver's strong sun wear first. Limewash is refreshed rather than repainted.

Can painted brick be returned to natural brick?

Sometimes, but it's slow and expensive, and it can damage the brick. Chemical strippers and careful low-pressure rinsing work on some walls. Abrasive blasting removes the hard outer face and should be avoided. Angi's Denver data puts paint stripping at $0.50 to $2.05 per square foot before repairs. On many houses, repainting with a brick-tone masonry coating or stain is the practical choice.

What paint should be used on exterior brick?

A vapor-permeable masonry coating: a breathable 100% acrylic masonry paint over a masonry primer where the label requires one, or a mineral silicate paint on bare masonry. Avoid ordinary house paint and heavy elastomerics on brick that can get wet from behind. Whatever you use, check the label's minimum temperature, which matters on cold brick in a Denver fall.

Do I need permission to paint my brick house in Denver?

Not usually, unless your house is a designated landmark, sits in a historic district or is governed by an HOA. Denver's updated landmark design guidelines (February 2026) say not to paint masonry that was never painted, so contact Denver Landmark Preservation first if that applies to you. Many HOAs also restrict painting brick accents, so check your design guidelines.

Is limewash better than paint for brick?

For bare brick, limewash is often the better choice. It's highly breathable, soaks in rather than forming a film, and ages softly instead of peeling. The trade-offs are a less uniform look and a refresh every several years. It won't bond over existing latex paint, so painted brick needs a masonry paint instead.
