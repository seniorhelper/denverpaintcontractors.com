Painting older Denver homes: Victorians, Denver Squares and bungalows

Century-old porches, deep eaves and a dozen coats of paint. How to repaint the outside of an older Denver house so it lasts, and what the city does and does not review.

By Denver Paint ContractorsPublished October 30, 202610 minute read

Short answer

Older Denver houses need prep matched to their type: Victorians have layered trim and many edges, Denver Squares have deep eaves and big porches, and bungalows expose rafter tails and porch piers to the weather. All of them predate 1978, so lead-safe practices and Colorado’s seven-day pamphlet rule apply. Denver landmark review does not include paint color, but painting never-painted brick in a district should be checked first. Book in winter so scraping and priming start on the first good spring weeks.

The three house types we paint most

A lot of Denver’s older housing falls into a few familiar shapes. Each has its own weak spots, and the weak spots decide the prep.

  • Victorians (roughly 1880s to 1900s). Queen Anne and related styles with wood siding or brick, decorative trim, turned porch posts and spindles, brackets and multiple roof planes. Lots of edges, lots of layers.
  • Denver Squares. The local name for the Foursquare: two stories, square plan, hipped roof with a central dormer, wide eaves often with brackets, and a front porch with columns or square posts. History Colorado notes most Colorado Foursquares went up in the first three decades of the twentieth century. Many are brick with painted wood eaves, porches and windows.
  • Bungalows (about 1900 to 1930). Low gabled roofs, deep overhanging eaves, often exposed rafter tails, and a broad front porch on tapered piers or thick columns. Brick or wood, with a lot of exposed wood at the roofline.

Under all of it is the layer problem. A new coat is only as attached as the oldest coat beneath it, so on a century-old house the prep is the job. For why paint fails on Colorado houses in general, see why exterior paint fails in Colorado.

Lead: assume it is there

Every house in this guide predates 1978. Colorado’s Regulation 19 requires the EPA Renovate Right pamphlet at least seven days before renovation that disturbs 2 square feet or more of painted surface, with a signed receipt, and federal lead-safe work practices apply: containment on the ground, wet scraping, no open-flame burning, and a cleaning check at the end. Details are in our lead-safe painting guide.

Historic districts: what review covers

Denver has 52 historic districts and nearly 350 individual landmarks, according to Historic Denver. The good news for painting: Historic Denver’s guide states plainly that design review does not include paint color. Review applies to exterior work that needs a permit, such as windows, siding or additions. The one painting question that does come up is masonry: Denver’s updated design guidelines advise against painting masonry that was never painted, so talk to Landmark Preservation before painting bare brick. Our brick guide covers the options.

Choosing colors that suit the house

Older houses look best when the color scheme follows the architecture. Victorians can carry three or more colors that pick out the trim; Denver Squares usually read best with a calm body, a contrasting trim on the eaves and porch, and a darker window sash; bungalows suit earthy bodies with the rafter tails and brackets highlighted. Our color selection page has swatches tested in Colorado light.

When to book

Book in winter, paint in spring. Some exterior products can go on in the cold: Sherwin-Williams lists Duration Exterior as applicable down to 35°F and Benjamin Moore lists Aura Exterior at 35°F to 100°F. But scraping and priming old wood wants dry, mild weeks, and the first of those fill fast. Booking now gets the walkthrough, the lead plan and the colors settled before the season starts. Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Do I need permission to paint my house in a Denver historic district?

Not for paint color. Historic Denver's guide to living in a district says design review does not include paint color. Review applies to exterior work that needs a permit, and Denver's 2026 design guidelines advise against painting masonry that was never painted, so check with Landmark Preservation before painting bare brick.

What is a Denver Square?

Denver's name for the American Foursquare: a two-story, square-plan house with a hipped roof, a central dormer, wide eaves and a front porch, often full width. History Colorado notes most Colorado Foursquares were built in the first three decades of the twentieth century.

How do you handle lead paint on an old house?

Anything built before 1978 is treated as lead-era. Colorado's Regulation 19 requires the EPA Renovate Right pamphlet at least seven days before work that disturbs 2 square feet or more of painted surface, and we use lead-safe containment, scraping and cleanup.

When should I book exterior painting on an older house?

Book in winter and paint in spring. Some exterior paints, such as Sherwin-Williams Duration and Benjamin Moore Aura, can be applied down to 35°F, but older wood needs dry, mild days for scraping and priming, so the first good weeks of spring are the target.

Should I paint my unpainted brick?

Usually not. Most Denver brick is best left bare with the trim painted. Our brick guide explains when painting makes sense.

Why does paint fail first on old porches and eaves?

Because that is where water and sun concentrate: porch floors and steps, the bottoms of columns, window sills and the south and west eaves. Those areas get the most scraping, priming and carpentry repair.
