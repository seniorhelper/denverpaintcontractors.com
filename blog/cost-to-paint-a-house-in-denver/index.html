How much does it cost to paint a house in Denver? The 2026 homeowner's price guide

Real ranges, the factors that move them, sample math you can run on your own home, and how to tell a fair quote from one that's quietly missing something.

The quick answer

If you want a single sentence: most Denver-area homeowners should expect exterior painting to fall somewhere around $1.55 to $4.10 per square foot, based on Angi's Denver cost data, and interior painting to be quoted somewhere around $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot. That's a wide range on purpose. Two houses with the exact same footprint can land thousands of dollars apart once you account for how tall they are, how much failing paint has to come off, and how much trim wraps every window.

So the honest answer is that the number that matters is the one built on your walls. The rest of this guide explains what goes into that number, so when a quote shows up you'll know whether it's fair, padded, or missing something important.

A quick note on where these figures come from. National cost sites survey contractor pricing and homeowner-reported project costs, and they update regularly. They're useful for a gut check. They aren't a price list for any specific company, including Bighorn Painting. Treat them as a starting point, not a promise.

Why painting prices in Denver look different

Denver isn't a sea-level market, and paint doesn't behave like it's in one. Several local conditions nudge prices up compared with milder places, and they're worth understanding because they're also the reasons a rock-bottom quote tends to fail here.

The sun is stronger at altitude

The World Health Organization notes that UV levels increase by roughly 10 percent for every 1,000 meters of altitude. Denver sits at about 1,600 meters, so sun-facing walls here take noticeably more UV than they would in a coastal city. UV breaks down paint binders over time, which shows up as fading and chalking. That's why product choice on south and west walls matters, and why higher-quality exterior lines earn their price along the Front Range.

Freeze-thaw cycles punish shortcuts

Colorado winters swing above and below freezing constantly. Water that gets behind paint through an open caulk joint or a crack freezes overnight, expands, and pushes the paint film away from the surface. Proper caulking, spot-priming and adhesion prep take time, and time is labor. A quote that skips it is cheaper for exactly one season.

A short, busy exterior season

According to the National Weather Service in Boulder, Denver's average last spring freeze is around May 5 and its average first fall freeze is around October 7. Exterior paint has minimum application temperatures printed on the label, and overnight lows matter. That compresses a lot of exterior work into late spring, summer and early fall, which is when good crews book up. We cover timing in depth in our month-by-month guide to painting in Colorado.

Housing stock with a lot of detail

Denver's older neighborhoods are full of bungalows, foursquares and Victorian-era homes with deep eaves, porch columns and trim on every edge. Trim is slow, careful work. A 1920s bungalow in Berkeley can take more hours than a newer two-story home with twice the siding simply because of the detail.

What exterior house painting costs in Denver

Exterior pricing is where the ranges swing the widest, because the outside of a house is where condition and access vary the most. Here's how to think about it.

Start with paintable area, not floor area

A common mistake is multiplying a per-square-foot price by the home's listed square footage. Exterior painting is priced on the surface being painted. A two-story house has far more wall than a ranch with the same floor area, and gables, dormers and bump-outs add more. Window and door openings reduce siding area but add trim. When a contractor measures your home, they're estimating the real surfaces, not the number on your listing.

These are illustrations to show how ranges work, not quotes. Plug in your own numbers to get a feel for the ballpark.

Why would the same house land near the low end for one homeowner and the high end for another? Almost always because of the factors below. A house with sound paint, simple trim and easy access sits near the bottom. A house with peeling trim, failed caulk, tall walls over a slope and lots of detail sits near the top.

What's usually included in a professional exterior price

  • Protecting windows, fixtures, landscaping, walkways and vehicles
  • Washing to remove dirt and chalk so paint can bond
  • Scraping loose and failing paint back to a sound edge
  • Sanding rough edges so old paint lines don't telegraph through
  • Caulking open joints around windows, doors and trim
  • Spot-priming bare wood and problem areas
  • Finish coats applied to the paint maker's specifications
  • Cleanup and a final walkthrough

If a quote doesn't mention most of these, ask. "Paint the house" on a single line tells you almost nothing.

What interior painting costs in Denver

Interior projects are easier to scope because conditions are controlled, and many can be estimated from photos. Pricing is commonly given per square foot, per room, or as a total for a set scope.

How interior pricing usually breaks down

  • Walls only is the simplest scope and the lowest per-room cost.
  • Walls and ceilings adds ladder work and more cutting in.
  • Walls, ceilings and trim adds baseboards, door and window casing, which is detail work.
  • Doors are often priced per door, front and back, and paneled doors take longer than flat ones.
  • Stairwells and vaulted ceilings need extra staging and time.

Interior factors that surprise people

Color changes are the big one. Going from a deep red accent wall to a soft white can take extra coats. Water and smoke stains need a stain-blocking primer first, or they bleed right back through. And patching matters: nail pops, cracks along drywall seams and old anchor holes all need filling and sanding before paint looks smooth. Dry Colorado air and seasonal movement make those small drywall issues common in metro homes.

The 12 factors that actually move a Denver painting quote

Every legitimate painting estimate is really a labor estimate plus materials. These are the things that change how many hours a job takes and what goes into it.

1. Height and access

Single-story homes are faster. Two-story walls, walkout basements that create three-story elevations, steep lots and gables need longer ladders, more setup and more careful work. Each move of a ladder is time.

2. Surface condition

This is the biggest swing factor on exteriors. Paint that's simply faded needs washing and fresh coats. Paint that's peeling, blistering or chalking heavily needs scraping, sanding and priming first. The worse the condition, the more prep hours before a single finish coat goes on.

3. Siding and surface type

Wood lap siding, fiber cement, stucco, brick, vinyl and aluminum all prep differently. Stucco cracks need attention and a masonry-appropriate coating. Vinyl going darker needs colors made for vinyl, like Sherwin-Williams VinylSafe or PPG SidingSafe collections, to avoid heat warping.

4. Trim, fascia and detail

Window trim, corner boards, fascia, soffits, shutters, porch columns and railings are slow, precise work. A house with lots of detail can cost more than a bigger house without it.

5. Caulk and minor repairs

Failed caulk has to come out before new caulk goes in. Minor wood filling is often part of prep, while rotted boards may need a carpenter first. Good estimates tell you which is which.

6. Number of coats

Coverage depends on the product, the color and the surface. Big color changes and porous surfaces often need more than you'd expect. Ask how many coats are included.

7. The paint line

Sherwin-Williams and PPG each make everything from budget lines to top-tier coatings. Higher-end exterior lines cost more per gallon and are generally chosen for better durability and color retention, which matters in Colorado sun. PPG's professional lines are sold through PPG paint stores and dealers, not big-box shelves, so "PPG" on a quote should come with a product name.

8. Color complexity

One body color and one trim color is simple. Multiple accent colors, a contrasting door, painted window sashes and two-tone schemes add cutting in and time.

9. Home age and lead paint

Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule requires certified firms and lead-safe work practices when painted surfaces in those homes are disturbed. Those practices add containment and cleanup steps, so expect it to affect price on older Denver homes.

10. Season and schedule

Peak exterior months are busy. Interior work in winter is often easier to schedule. Flexibility on dates can help, and it never hurts to ask about seasonal pricing.

11. HOA requirements

Covenant-controlled neighborhoods often require color approval, sometimes with samples. That's more about planning time than cost, but it can affect when work can start.

12. Furniture, access and occupancy

Interior jobs in fully furnished rooms take longer than empty ones. Clearing small items yourself can shave time. Tight spaces, pets and working around daily life all factor in.

Cabinets, decks and doors: how specialty projects are priced

Kitchen cabinet painting

Cabinet painting is priced by the number of doors, drawer fronts and linear feet of boxes, plus the level of prep and the finish. A proper cabinet job involves degreasing, scuff-sanding, a bonding primer and a sprayed cabinet-grade finish, with doors typically finished in a controlled area. It costs more per square foot than walls because it's detail work, but far less than new cabinetry when the boxes are solid.

Deck and fence staining

Staining is priced by square footage of deck boards, linear feet of railings and fence, and condition. Graying, splitting wood needs cleaning and brightening and sometimes sanding before stain can soak in. Waiting too long raises prep time and, eventually, turns maintenance into repairs. Our deck staining section covers what's involved.

Front doors and garage doors

Doors are often priced per door. A front door color change is one of the biggest curb-appeal updates for the money, especially before listing a home.

Red flags in a low painting quote

  • One-line scope. "Paint exterior, two colors" with no prep listed.
  • No product named. If they won't write down the paint line, they have room to swap in something cheaper.
  • Large cash-only deposit. Be cautious with big upfront cash demands and no paperwork.
  • No proof of insurance. If someone gets hurt on your property, you want coverage confirmed in writing.
  • Pressure to sign today. Countdown deals and "this price expires tonight" tactics are about urgency, not value.
  • No warranty, or a verbal one. A promise you can't point to is hard to enforce.
  • No reviews or a vanishing phone number. You want a company that will still be around if something needs attention later.

Legitimate ways to lower your painting cost

You don't need a gimmick coupon to save money. These actually work.

  • Bundle projects. Painting trim, a door and a few rooms in one visit means one setup and one cleanup.
  • Be flexible on timing. Interior work in winter is often easier to schedule.
  • Handle easy prep yourself. Clear rooms, move small furniture and pull art off walls. Leave scraping, sanding and priming to the pros.
  • Don't wait for failure. Repainting when paint is faded is cheaper than repainting after it's peeling and the wood is exposed.
  • Stain decks on schedule. Maintenance costs less than board replacement.
  • Put premium product where it matters. South and west walls benefit most from top-tier exterior coatings.

How to get an accurate painting estimate fast

Whether you're getting a virtual estimate or an in-person one, good information gets you a better number sooner.

For a virtual estimate from photos

  • Take a straight-on photo of each side of the house, or each wall of each room.
  • Add close-ups of problem areas: peeling trim, stains, cracks, failed caulk.
  • Include a wide shot that shows height and slope for exteriors.
  • Note the year the home was built and any HOA color rules.
  • List what you want painted and what you don't.

For an in-home estimate

  • Have a rough idea of colors, or ask for color help.
  • Point out anything that bothers you, even small things.
  • Ask what's included, how many coats, which product, and what the warranty covers.
  • Ask how scheduling and payment work.

Four real-world Denver scenarios, and what drives each price

Numbers are easier to understand with a house attached. These composite scenarios aren't quotes and don't describe specific customers. They show how the same cost factors play out on common Denver-area homes.

Scenario 1: A 1920s bungalow in Berkeley or Sunnyside

The footprint is modest, so you might expect a low price. But the house has a deep front porch with columns, exposed rafter tails, wide trim around every window and decades of paint layers. The south side is chalking heavily and several window sills are peeling down to bare wood. Built well before 1978, it may need lead-safe work practices when painted surfaces are disturbed. The biggest cost drivers here are trim detail, scraping and priming, and lead-safe containment and cleanup. It can easily cost more than a newer house twice its size.

Scenario 2: A 1990s two-story in Highlands Ranch

The siding is large and fairly simple, but the walls are tall, and the back of the house faces a slope that adds height. The original caulk has dried out, and a few hardboard siding boards near the ground are swollen from sprinklers. HRCA color review has to happen before work starts. The cost drivers are height and staging, caulk replacement, addressing damaged boards before paint, and making sure the approved colors are on paper before scheduling. Our Highlands Ranch page covers the approval side.

Scenario 3: An interior refresh in an Aurora ranch

The homeowner wants the living room, hallway and three bedrooms repainted from a dated tan to a soft white, plus ceilings. The walls have nail pops and a few old anchor holes, and one bedroom has a dark accent wall. The cost drivers are the dark-to-light color change that may need extra coats, patching and sanding, and ceilings. Because conditions are straightforward, this is a good candidate for a virtual estimate from photos. See more about painting in Aurora.

Scenario 4: A rental turnover in Littleton

A landlord needs the unit repainted quickly between tenants. Walls are scuffed, there's a small water stain on a ceiling from a fixed leak, and trim needs touch-ups. The cost drivers are speed and scheduling, stain-blocking primer on the ceiling, and a durable, washable finish that holds up to the next tenant. A tight, clearly written scope keeps this kind of job efficient.

How deposits and payments usually work

Payment terms vary by contractor, so read them before you sign. A clear estimate should spell out whether a deposit is required, when remaining payments are due, and what forms of payment are accepted. Be cautious with anyone who wants a large cash payment upfront with no written agreement, or who pressures you to pay before any work or materials are scheduled. A professional contract protects both you and the painter by making expectations clear. Bighorn Painting lays out the payment schedule on its written estimate and accepts cash, check and credit card.

Is a cheap paint job ever worth it?

Sometimes a lower-cost approach makes sense. If you're selling a house in a month and simply need clean, neutral walls, a basic interior refresh can be the right call. If a shed or detached garage just needs a cosmetic update, a simpler scope may be fine.

What rarely pays off is a cheap exterior on the house you plan to live in. In Colorado, skipped prep and bargain paint tend to show up as peeling, chalking and fading within a few seasons, and the next repaint then needs even more prep. Paying for the same house twice is the most expensive way to paint it. If budget is tight, it's usually smarter to reduce the scope, such as painting the worst two elevations and the trim this year, than to cut the prep on the whole house.

The bottom line

Denver painting prices vary widely because houses vary widely. Market data gives you a useful range, roughly $1.55 to $4.10 per square foot for exteriors and around $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot for interiors, but the only number worth comparing is a written one built on your home, with prep, products, coats and warranty spelled out. In Colorado's sun and freeze-thaw, the right paint job done once almost always costs less than the cheap one done twice.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to paint the exterior of a house in Denver?

Angi's Denver cost data puts exterior house painting at roughly $1.55 to $4.10 per square foot of paintable surface. Stories and height, surface condition, siding type, trim detail and the paint line decide where a specific home lands.

How much does interior painting cost in Denver?

Interior painting in Denver is commonly quoted between $1.50 and $3.50 per square foot, and many projects are priced per room. Ceilings, trim, doors, repairs and big color changes add to the cost.

Why do painting quotes vary so much for the same house?

Quotes vary because contractors include different amounts of prep, different paint lines, different numbers of coats and different warranties. Compare quotes line by line on those items instead of by total alone.

Is it cheaper to paint a house in winter in Denver?

Exterior painting depends on warm weather, so winter exteriors are rare. Interior work runs year-round, and winter is often easier to schedule. Ask about seasonal pricing when you request an estimate.

Does an older Denver home cost more to paint?

Often, yes. Older homes tend to have more trim detail and more layers of failing paint, and homes built before 1978 may require EPA lead-safe work practices when painted surfaces are disturbed.

How much does it cost to paint kitchen cabinets in Denver?

Cabinet painting is priced by the number of doors, drawer fronts and cabinet boxes, plus prep and finish. It costs more per square foot than walls but typically far less than replacing cabinets when the boxes are solid.

Can I get a painting estimate from photos?

Yes. Many interior, cabinet, deck and simpler exterior projects can be estimated virtually from photos. Bighorn Painting offers free virtual and in-home estimates.

What should a painting quote include?

A complete quote lists the surfaces, prep steps, paint line and sheen, number of coats, protection and cleanup, schedule, payment terms, insurance and the warranty in writing.
