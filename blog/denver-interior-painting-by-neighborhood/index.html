Interior painting

Denver interior painting, neighborhood by neighborhood

A 1910 Capitol Hill foursquare and a 2015 Central Park house can have the same square footage and need completely different interior work. Here is what each era of Denver housing asks for.

By Denver Paint ContractorsPublished October 30, 20269 minute read

Short answer

Denver interiors differ by housing era more than by size. Pre-1940 homes in neighborhoods like Capitol Hill, the Highlands and Wash Park usually need plaster repair, bonding primer over old enamel and lead-safe practices; mid-century ranches bring textured ceilings that fall under Colorado’s 32-square-foot asbestos rule if disturbed; condo buildings add contractor rules; and newer homes need builder flat paint and nail pops fixed. Winter is a good time for all of it if rooms stay at or above 50°F and ventilation is planned.

Why the neighborhood matters inside

Denver’s neighborhoods were mostly built in waves, so the address is a good shortcut to what is behind the paint. Bungalows in Washington Park, foursquares in Capitol Hill, Tudors in Park Hill, post-war ranches in Harvey Park, and newer homes in Central Park built on the old Stapleton airport land since the early 2000s. Each era has its own walls, its own trim and its own problems.

Older houses: plaster, trim and lead

Pre-war Denver interiors are usually plaster, not drywall. Plaster cracks along stairwells and above doors as the house moves, and a skim of joint compound over a moving crack comes back within a season. We re-attach loose plaster and repair with materials that bond to it before any paint goes on. Trim and doors often carry oil-based enamel under later latex; that needs a bonding primer, or the new paint peels off in strips.

Then lead. In housing built before 1978, Colorado’s Regulation 19 requires the EPA Renovate Right pamphlet at least seven days before work that disturbs 2 square feet or more of painted surface, and lead-safe practices apply. Our lead-safe painting guide explains what that looks like day to day. Radiators are common in these houses too: the heat goes off and the radiator cools before the wall behind it is painted.

Mid-century homes: texture and paneling

Ranches and split-levels from the 1950s to 1970s bring textured ceilings, wood paneling and original trim. Painting over intact texture is fine. Scraping or sanding it is a different matter: in a single-family home, disturbing more than 32 square feet of material that could contain asbestos requires inspection by a Colorado-certified inspector first. Our popcorn ceiling page covers the rule. Paneling paints well once it is cleaned, scuffed and primed with a primer that blocks the wood’s tannins.

Condos and apartments

Many Capitol Hill and downtown buildings set rules for contractors: working hours, elevator reservations, protection of hallways and a certificate of insurance on file. Ask the manager first. Inside the unit, the job is about staging: fewer rooms open at once, furniture moved within a small footprint, and cleanup every day so you can live there while we work.

Newer homes: builder paint and nail pops

Homes built in the last 25 years are easier to prep but have their own pattern. Builders usually leave flat paint that marks the first time it is wiped, and framing shrinkage shows up as nail pops and corner cracks in the first years. We fix the pops, prime the repairs and switch high-traffic rooms to a washable finish. Drywall work on its own has a page: drywall repair in Denver.

Planning it for winter

Winter is the easiest time to book interior work in Denver: exterior crews are off the siding and the calendar opens up. Two things matter more in the cold months. First, temperature: many interior paints list a 50°F minimum, so heat closed-off rooms, basements and sunrooms beforehand. Second, air: the EPA notes that many VOCs are up to ten times higher indoors than outdoors and recommends increasing ventilation when using products that emit them, so we use low-VOC products, run exhaust fans and open a window in the work room on milder afternoons. Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Does the neighborhood really change an interior paint job?

Yes, because it tells you the housing era. A 1910s house with plaster walls, deep trim and possible lead paint needs different prep from a 2010s house with builder drywall and flat paint, even if the rooms are the same size.

Is it OK to paint interiors in a Denver winter?

Yes. Keep rooms at or above the paint's minimum temperature, often 50°F, and plan ventilation. The EPA recommends increasing ventilation when using products that emit VOCs.

Do condo buildings have rules for painters?

Many do: work hours, elevator reservations, protection of common areas and proof of insurance. Ask your building manager before booking, and we will work to their rules.

How do you paint around radiators?

The heat is shut off and the radiator allowed to cool, the wall behind it is painted with a long radiator brush, and the radiator itself is only painted with a product rated for the heat if you want it refinished.

My house is older. What should I ask a painter?

Ask about lead: in pre-1978 homes Colorado requires the Renovate Right pamphlet at least seven days before work that disturbs 2 square feet or more of painted surface. And ask how they will repair plaster, not just patch over it.
